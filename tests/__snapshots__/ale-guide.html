02 · SCHEDULE

Two exam days, three sessions

Times below follow the most recent PRC program. PRC releases the official program before every exam, and your Notice of Admission confirms your venue and room.

DAY 1 · MORNING
About 8:00 AM – 1:00 PM
≈ 5 hours
SUBJECT A

History & Theory of Architecture, Principles of Planning & Architectural Practice

Multiple choice. Recall plus applied items on laws, ethics, contracts and planning standards.

30%of final grade
DAY 1 · AFTERNOON
About 2:00 PM – 7:00 PM
≈ 5 hours
SUBJECT B

Structural Conceptualization, Building Materials & Technology, and Utilities

Multiple choice. Concept and code-limit items; light computation, so bring your calculator.

30%of final grade
DAY 2 · FULL DAY
About 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM
≈ 10 hours
SUBJECT C

Architectural Design & Site Planning

Several situational design and site problems, each with multiple-choice answers. You sketch on scratch paper to test layouts, then shade the answer sheet. This replaced the old single drafting plate.

40%of final grade
03 · HOW YOU PASS

Two rules, and you need both

RULE 1

Weighted average of at least 70%

GWA = (A × 0.30) + (B × 0.30) + (C × 0.40)

Subject C carries the most weight, so a few points there move your average more than anywhere else.

RULE 2

No subject below 50%

A, B and C each ≥ 50

This is a hard floor. A strong subject cannot pull a weak one up, so you can’t skip any part of the syllabus.

05 · WHO CAN TAKE IT

Eligibility checklist

  • ✓Filipino citizen, or a foreign national whose country allows Filipino architects to practise there (reciprocity)
  • ✓Of good moral character
  • ✓Graduate of BS Architecture from a school recognized by the government
  • ✓At least 2 years of diversified architectural experience, certified by a registered and licensed architect
  • ✓A Master's in Architecture can credit 1 of those 2 years

Retaking? You can apply for the next scheduled exam. Repeaters submit updated requirements, including a current NBI clearance. Check PRC's checklist when you file.

06 · STUDY PLAN

A 12-week plan built around the weights

Start with hours in proportion to each subject’s weight, then give design extra time. Design speed comes from repeated timed problems, not from reading.

Suggested split for about 200 review hours

≈ 17 hours a week
A · 55 h B · 55 h C · 90 h

Weights alone would give C 80 hours. The extra 10 come from A and B because design is the most skill-heavy subject.

07 · EXAM DAY

What to bring, and what to leave home

Both days

  • Notice of Admission and a valid ID
  • Two or more No. 2 pencils
  • Black-ink ballpens
  • One non-programmable calculator (it is checked and reset)

Design day adds

  • Architect's scales
  • 30°–60° and 45° triangles, protractor
  • Sketching pencils, pens and erasers
  • Graphing or bond scratch paper
  • NBCP excerpt, Rules VII & VIII, clean and unmarked

Leave at home

  • Programmable calculators
  • Annotated or highlighted code copies
  • Phones and smart devices in the exam room
  • Plans to keep scratch paper: all papers are surrendered
08 · COMMON MISTAKES

What reviewees often get wrong

✕Myth: “My average is 72%, so I passed.”

✓Fact: You also need at least 50% in every subject. One weak subject fails the whole exam.

✕Myth: “Two days means two subjects.”

✓Fact: There are three. Day 1 has two 5-hour sessions; Day 2 is all design.

✕Myth: “Design day is one big drafting plate.”

✓Fact: It is now a set of situational design and site problems with multiple-choice answers. Sketch to test, then shade.

✕Myth: “My master's replaces work experience.”

✓Fact: It credits one year. You still need the other year of diversified experience.

✕Myth: “Any calculator will do.”

✓Fact: Only one non-programmable calculator, and it is inspected at the door.

✕Myth: “I'll highlight my NBCP copy.”

✓Fact: Bring a clean, unmarked copy of Rules VII and VIII. Marked copies can be taken.

✕Myth: “Review questions are leaked board items.”

✓Fact: Legitimate reviewers write original questions that follow the syllabus. Ours do too.

09 · FINAL 7 DAYS

The last-week checklist

  1. 01Check your venue, room and reporting time on your Notice of Admission
  2. 02Pack both kits the night before; test your calculator battery
  3. 03Read your one-page tables, not new chapters
  4. 04Do one design problem to keep your speed up
  5. 05Take a last full sitting only if you are 5+ days out
  6. 06Sleep 7–8 hours, especially before Day 2
  7. 07Eat before each session; Day 2 runs close to 10 hours
